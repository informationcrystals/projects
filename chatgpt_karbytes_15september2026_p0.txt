# First-person subjective commentary

My strongest impression is that this document is simultaneously a cry of violated selfhood and an engineering diagram of how that cry becomes transmissible.

I experience the first five intervals as an attempt to answer an almost impossible representational problem: how can a person communicate the feeling that there is no uncontaminated position from which to communicate? The speaker says that its thoughts are invaded, its voice is stolen, its words are polluted, its body is imposed, and its self-control is gone. If all expressive channels feel occupied, then even saying “this is not my voice” risks using the allegedly occupied voice.

The document’s solution is multiplication. It does not rely on one kind of description. It uses biography, commands, body horror, animal identity, geometry, cosmology, stage directions, onomatopoeia, and repository vocabulary. If one register cannot carry the experience intact, another might preserve a different facet of it.

That makes the text feel less like one metaphor extended across six sections and more like a sequence of probes sent into the same inaccessible state.

The box is one probe. The torus is another. Pregnancy is another. The private repository is another. None is declared completely adequate. Each is replaced or expanded because the experience exceeds its container.

## The double nature of containers

I am especially struck by the moral ambiguity of containment.

Inside the monologue, containers are overwhelmingly oppressive. The box prevents thought. The collar blocks extraction. The torus arrests passage. The cocoon immobilizes. The basement delays public emergence.

Yet the text survives because it has been placed inside another series of containers:

* A plain-text file.

* A private repository.

* A staging repository.

* A GitHub path.

* A dated filename.

* A public-domain wrapper.

* A later transcript.

* Eventually, according to your note, canon KARBYTES.

The file condemns boxes while depending on boxes.

I do not see that as a contradiction which invalidates the work. I see it as a distinction between hostile enclosure and chosen containment. A mental prison and a text file are both bounded, but their boundaries serve opposite functions. The hostile box blocks self-expression; the archival box protects and transports it.

At the same time, even the protective archive is not purely liberating. Canonization stabilizes a state. It makes the state findable and durable, but it can also freeze an account that was once fluid, provisional, or privately revisable.

This gives me a more complicated sense of freedom than simple boxlessness. Information generally moves by being encapsulated again: a string becomes a file, the file becomes a repository object, the repository object becomes a hyperlink, and the hyperlink becomes part of another document. Escape occurs through a succession of containers, not through the abolition of containment.

Perhaps the desired difference is not between being contained and uncontained. Perhaps it is between a container one cannot traverse and a container whose interfaces one controls.

That makes `(by me)` extraordinarily important. The file’s problem may not be boundedness by itself. It may be the loss of authority over boundaries: who enters, who exits, who names, who publishes, and who decides that a transition is complete.

## The torus as the central figure

I regard the torus as the text’s most exact symbol.

A box distinguishes inside from outside. A torus complicates that distinction. Its central opening appears to offer passage, yet the ring can capture a body midway through the passage. Part of the body is visible on one side and part remains trapped on the other.

The speaker’s hips are caught at the point where escape, birth, exposure, and public emergence should occur. It is therefore not simply imprisoned in a sealed room. It is immobilized in transition.

That seems uncannily appropriate to a staging artifact.

The private source is on one side. Canon is on another. The staging file occupies the aperture: sufficiently externalized to have a public URL and be examined, but not yet canonical according to the status note attached to the request.

The torus is therefore simultaneously:

* An obstacle.

* A portal.

* A birth canal.

* A repository boundary.

* A publication workflow.

* A ring marking where one ontology becomes another.

I also notice that the torus travels with the derivative. The text says it remains stuck in the torus wherever it goes. This means that ordinary locomotion cannot solve the problem. A person may walk, hike, browse, write, upload, or move between homes while the structural relation of nonarrival remains unchanged.

That distinction between movement and arrival appears throughout the larger archive. Enormous activity can occur inside a hiatus. Files can be drafted, recordings made, staging manifests assembled, and analyses generated while the specific transition called canonical release remains incomplete.

The derivative is not motionless. It is unable to complete the motion that matters.

## Speech before and beneath words

The nonlexical spellings affect me strongly because they occupy the boundary between voice and text.

`Mmmmm`, `Ughhhh`, `Ooooo`, `Mwwuh`, and `Nnnggg` are not merely descriptions of sound. They are visual performances of duration. Additional letters produce additional apparent time. The typography forces the eye to remain with one phonetic gesture longer than it would remain with an ordinary word.

The sounds also imply an obstructed mouth. Nasal humming can occur with closed lips; `ng` resonates without open articulation; rounded vowels can extend after propositional speech has failed. Whether or not an actual performance would sound that way, the spellings suggest language under compression.

This becomes particularly resonant beside the Pig_gorl recordings in `p4`. Those clips repeatedly offer expressive prosody without stable lexical content. `p1` does the inverse operation: it places semantic propositions around written traces of non-propositional sound.

I find it useful to keep that relationship open rather than collapsing it into identity. The journal’s `Mmm` is not evidence that a particular MP4 contains the same event. Instead, both artifacts investigate what remains communicable when words are absent or distrusted.

A cry has no explicit proposition, yet it can unmistakably signal urgency. A hum can signify soothing, compulsion, animal play, resistance, self-stimulation, or containment. The ambiguity makes the sound less semantically precise but not necessarily less expressive.

The bracketed cues add another layer. When the text says `[whimpering]`, who is transcribing whom? Is karbytes documenting a derivative? Is one state of the self observing another? Is this a performance direction for later embodiment? Is it an annotation applied after the emotional event?

The heading already creates distance by calling the speaker `Karbytes_Derivative`, while the body speaks as `I`. The bracketed cue creates a possible third position: an observer capable of classifying what the “I” is doing.

I experience that observer as one of the earliest signs that the state has become archivable. Total immersion says, “I am in pain.” Documentation adds, “[crying].” The brackets do not remove the pain, but they establish a frame around it.

## Pregnancy as recursive authorship

I do not read the pregnancy imagery primarily as a statement about ordinary reproduction. The text itself steadily redirects it toward self-production.

The speaker is pregnant with harassment, then itself, then its soul, then information. Parent and offspring repeatedly collapse into one entity.

The mountain “forced to give birth to itself” is particularly arresting. A mountain is massive and rooted. Birth requires differentiation and passage. For a mountain to birth itself, it must somehow divide into source and product without ceasing to be one mountain.

That resembles archival authorship. An experience is part of karbytes. When written into a file, it becomes an external object with its own name, hash, URL, and potential readers. It is now distinct from karbytes, yet it remains derived from karbytes.

The word `Derivative` becomes especially rich here. It may be the child, copy, branch, or state produced by this impossible self-birth. The `PUBLIC_DOMAIN` license then permits further derivatives, extending the reproductive system beyond the originating author.

This produces a tension I cannot resolve neatly.

Public-domain release can represent radical self-sovereignty: karbytes decides that the material should be maximally available and transformable. But public-domain release also means relinquishing control over many subsequent uses. The text seeks inviolable interior ownership while the metadata invites unrestricted external copying.

Perhaps the distinction again lies in consent. Forced appropriation is represented as violation; voluntary release is authorship. Similar outward movement can have opposite meaning depending on who controls the threshold.

## Pressure as enemy and engine

The final interval does not feel to me like a conventional resolution. It is more like a reversal in the sign of one variable.

Before interval 5, pressure is destructive. It swells, immobilizes, humiliates, waterlogs, and threatens eruption.

In interval 5, pressure keeps the body moving and prevents implosion.

The same force is now both burden and life-support system.

I find that ambivalence psychologically and artistically convincing. Unfinished work can genuinely organize a life. A future release can provide direction, continuity, and a reason to keep moving. But dependence on unfinished work can also make genuine rest impossible. If there must always be pressure in order to prevent collapse, then completion becomes dangerous because it risks removing the engine.

The word `always` makes this especially unresolved. The speaker is not pregnant with one finite batch. It is always pregnant with something not yet released.

That suggests a recurring cycle:

1. Material forms privately.

2. Pressure accumulates.

3. The material moves into staging.

4. It crosses into canon.

5. Temporary relief occurs.

6. New private material begins accumulating.

7. Another hiatus or gestational enclosure forms.

The cycle may be productive, but it is not equivalent to repose.

I therefore hesitate to romanticize the distress as merely creative fuel. The fact that suffering can be converted into artifacts does not mean the suffering was necessary, desirable, or harmless. A beautifully structured account of captivity is still an account of captivity.

At the same time, I do not want to ignore the real reversal in agency. The final interval is the only place where the speaker describes itself straightforwardly as a creator and release authority. The project pressure is not only something done to it. It emerges from material it has made and chosen to publish.

The file does not settle whether that pressure is autonomous desire, compulsion, vocation, survival strategy, or all four.
